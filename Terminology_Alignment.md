# Terminology alignment

Use the [Double-FW terminology repository](https://github.com/Double-FW/Terminology-and-Principles) in its stated scope. The inspected revision is `219b5feddd271bcdf70ea0ac0c4bc5c62fe9a0f4`; its [governance](https://github.com/Double-FW/Terminology-and-Principles/blob/219b5feddd271bcdf70ea0ac0c4bc5c62fe9a0f4/GOVERNANCE.md) and [owner decisions](https://github.com/Double-FW/Terminology-and-Principles/blob/219b5feddd271bcdf70ea0ac0c4bc5c62fe9a0f4/review/DECISIONS.md) establish authority and resolution boundaries. Most individual definitions remain proposed. Publishing this alignment does not approve every glossary entry or adopt an organisation's accommodation policy.

## Meanings and authority

| Term or distinction | Use in accommodation work |
| --- | --- |
| Accessibility | Address barriers to the scoped activity and experience; do not infer success from an installation, tick or one participant. |
| Inclusion | Consider participation, influence, dignity and experience alongside access. It is not interchangeable with technical conformance. |
| Reasonable adjustment | Preserve the meaning, scope and decision requirements of the actual applicable law. A general recommendation is not automatically a legally required or sufficient adjustment. |
| Independent use | Support the person's preferred level of independence and chosen assistance. Do not treat requesting help as personal failure. |
| Equivalent/comparable experience | State the comparison across intended outcomes, effort, privacy, availability, control and dignity. Different formats or routes need not be identical, but their practical consequences must be examined (D02). |
| Satisfactory / satisfaction | Satisfactory is the proposed Double-FW experience principle; satisfaction is a person's assessment. A high rating cannot cancel a serious barrier, safety issue or unmet external requirement (D01). |
| Emotional lenses | Feeling Seen, Feeling Capable, Feeling Confident, Feeling Safe and Feeling Valued guide research questions and intended qualities. Do not assign these feelings from a designer's intention or a technical pass (D03). |
| Sensory / cognitive / physical coverage | P/N/C are overlapping checklist tags. They are neither diagnostic categories nor prevalence estimates. Preserve identity and language preferences rather than inferring needs from diagnosis. |

Use adopted Double-FW meanings for its methodologies, explaining a departure from another approach. Preserve source-owned meanings when quoting or assessing legislation, building/fire requirements, professional standards or contracts (D08). A Double-FW source preference cannot change a statute or justify an unsafe installation. Record a conflict, its scope and the competent decision needed rather than silently merging incompatible requirements.

If referencing the Cognitive UX principles, use their `CP-01`–`CP-10` identifiers and name the owning source, because bare CP has other meanings (D04). Use ACT-style procedural test or user-oriented task script for local procedures; use W3C ACT only where that is the actual source (D05). Do not imply that a building walkthrough is a W3C-approved test.

## Evidence, progress and decisions

Preserve observation, participant account, estimate, hypothesis, agent inference, illustrative/synthetic example, proposal and actual approval as different records (D07). A supplier claim needs its source and verification status. A generated explanation or filled template is not an inspection or human decision.

| Evaluation result | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Verified | Relevant evidence supports the stated criterion in the recorded version, place and conditions. It is not universal suitability or certification. |
| Failed | Evidence shows the scoped criterion was not met. |
| Unknown | Available evidence has not established the result. |
| Not tested | The specified check was not performed. |
| Not applicable | The criterion does not apply to the stated scope, with a recorded rationale and required human review. |

Keep **unresolved** as an open issue/decision with an underlying result, owner and next action where known. Keep **installed**, **completed** or other progress labels separate from evaluation results. Keep occupant activity outcomes separate again: an individually verified feature may form part of a journey that was not tested or was not achieved.

Approval requires actual authority, scope, decision and evidence; do not infer it from a Git merge, repository publication or dataset export. Dates describe different events: retrieving a source or exporting a file does not renew an older inspection, legal review or product check.

## Experience and identity

Investigate comprehension, orientation, control, rest, participation, effort, privacy and dignity alongside functional access. Compare the actual intended journey and record chosen versus unavoidable assistance. Satisfaction and feeling safe are participant findings, not proof of building safety, WCAG conformance, cybersecurity or compatibility with all assistive technologies (D03/D06).

Respect Deaf and neurodivergent identities and people's preferred language. Do not diagnose from behaviour, equipment or a requested room. Ask only for task-relevant information and preserve appropriate privacy and consent arrangements.
