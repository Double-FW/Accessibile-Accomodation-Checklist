# Accessible accommodation checklist

Design and manage accommodation as one journey: understanding the site, reaching a destination, using rooms and facilities, resting, participating and obtaining support. Offer choice and the person's preferred level of independence, with dignity and privacy. This applies to accommodation planning and review; actors and visiting professionals are examples, not the only occupants.

## How to use this checklist

Each item is a design or operational recommendation to assess for the actual project. A tick means the recorded assessment and action are complete for the stated scope; it is not a compliance certificate or proof that every occupant can use the accommodation. Record applicable obligations and accepted project criteria separately. Do not turn an unqualified number or product example into a universal building requirement.

Keep the coverage tags used in earlier versions:

- **P — Physical access:** movement, reach, strength, dexterity and use of spaces/equipment.
- **N — Non-visual/sensory access:** information and sensory conditions across visual, auditory and tactile routes, including Deaf, hard-of-hearing, blind, partially sighted and Deafblind access needs.
- **C — Cognitive/neuroinclusive access:** orientation, comprehension, processing demands, predictability and sensory preferences.

Tags overlap. They identify aspects to consider, not diagnostic categories, complete population coverage or counts of distinct people. Ask about relevant needs and preferences rather than inferring them from diagnosis. Item identifiers `ACCOM-01`–`ACCOM-32` are local checklist references introduced in this version, not standards clauses or certification criteria. A compound item may need several project tests; retain its parent ID and evidence trail.

### Scope, requirements and competent decisions

Confirm the country, building use/classification, new-build/refurbishment/existing-building context, occupancy and work in scope with the responsible professionals. In England, select the applicable [Approved Document M volume and amendments](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/access-to-and-use-of-buildings-approved-document-m): dwellings and buildings other than dwellings have different guidance. Approved Documents guide Building Regulations compliance; this checklist does not establish legal compliance or choose the applicable route.

Consider equality/adjustment duties separately from building design guidance. The [EHRC services code](https://www.equalityhumanrights.com/guidance/codes-practice/code-practice-services-public-functions-and-associations/chapter-seven) is a starting point for its stated service context; employment, premises and other situations need their own applicable sources. Do not assume England-specific guidance applies to another jurisdiction.

Use BS 8300-1, BS 8300-2 and PAS 6463 as relevant professional references. Check editions, applicability and actual clauses through lawful access; a catalogue page is not the full standard. Record the source, edition, clause, measurements and decision that support each accepted technical criterion. The responsible building-control, access, structural, electrical and fire-safety professionals must determine matters within their competence.

## 1. Knowing where you are

- [ ] **ACCOM-01 (P/N/C)** Provide accessible pre-arrival and on-site information showing step-free routes, lifts and their operation, toilets, quiet routes, drop-off points, assistance-dog facilities and key destinations. Include rehearsal/performance spaces where relevant. Offer structured text or accessible HTML/documents alongside visual maps; do not assume that a visual PDF or an attached Word file is accessible.

- [ ] **ACCOM-02 (N/C)** Use clear, contrasting, plain-language signs, consistent names/symbols and predictable placement. Make room numbers and key destinations findable, with reachable tactile/embossed information and Braille where appropriate to the audience and specification. Preserve relevant language preferences; Braille alone does not serve every blind person.

- [ ] **ACCOM-03 (N/C)** Orient on-site maps to the viewing direction where appropriate, identify “you are here” and useful landmarks, and offer accessible route descriptions. Any journey-time estimate should identify the route and assumptions rather than imply one pace suits everyone. Consider professionally designed tactile maps at decision points.

- [ ] **ACCOM-04 (N/C)** Consider optional electronic location markers, such as NaviLens, where they support the intended audience. Assess language, device, installation, maintenance and offline/failure conditions. They supplement physical wayfinding and human assistance; no visitor should need one particular phone or app to use an essential route.

- [ ] **ACCOM-05 (N/C)** Match zone names, numbers and route cues across maps, signs and relevant doors/walls. Use colour with words, symbols or another distinguishable cue, not colour alone. Avoid a pattern or contrast treatment that creates a misleading apparent step, hole or obstruction.

- [ ] **ACCOM-06 (C/N)** Offer an accessible pre-arrival sensory description covering lighting, likely noise, smells, busy periods, quiet spaces and route appearance. Keep it current and identify temporary works or changes that affect the described experience.

## 2. The route to where you want to get to

- [ ] **ACCOM-07 (P)** Plan continuous step-free routes between relevant arrival points, accommodation and facilities, including rehearsal/performance destinations where relevant. Compare distance, effort, opening hours, safety and dignity; avoid unnecessary segregation or substantially worse access without recording and addressing the barrier.

- [ ] **ACCOM-08 (P/N)** Specify and verify doors as an assembled access route: effective clear opening, approach and manoeuvring space, thresholds, opening force, reach, controls, timing and safe use from either side. Select dimensions and control heights from the applicable professional specification and intended users' needs; no single width guarantees passage for the “largest powered chair”. Where powered operation is appropriate, place identifiable controls outside the door sweep and test reachable operation with limited dexterity. Determine closing and hold-open arrangements with the fire/access professionals. Do not wedge a fire door, disable a closer or add a hold-open function that conflicts with its approved fire-safety arrangements.

- [ ] **ACCOM-09 (P)** Provide and verify suitable corridor, lobby, turning and passing spaces for the scoped mobility aids, companions and assistance arrangements. Keep routes free of furniture, cables, bins, charging equipment and temporary obstructions. A clear door opening alone does not demonstrate a usable route.

- [ ] **ACCOM-10 (N/P)** Provide accessible lift information and operation: spoken and visual floor/direction information, identifiable tactile controls and relevant Braille, with suitable reach and feedback. Braille markings on a featureless touch-sensitive surface do not by themselves make it usable. Test the actual controls and assistance route; optional electronic markers do not replace them. Ordinary passenger-lift access does not establish suitability for evacuation.

- [ ] **ACCOM-11 (N/C)** Coordinate wayfinding cues, floor finishes, contrast and step edges with a competent access designer. Avoid confusing visual patterns, improvised raised strips and tactile cues that create trip risks or conflict with recognised conventions. Verify intended cues under actual lighting and use conditions.

- [ ] **ACCOM-12 (N/P)** Provide suitable lighting, glare control, handrails and identifiable step edges where relevant. Avoid unnecessary sudden lighting changes and confusing floor/wall patterns. User lighting controls must not compromise required emergency lighting or safe circulation.

- [ ] **ACCOM-13 (C)** Identify quieter or lower-stimulation route choices where feasible and explain their availability on accessible maps and descriptions. Do not label a route “quiet” without considering actual use, time and temporary conditions.

- [ ] **ACCOM-14 (P/N/C)** Agree accessible emergency arrangements with the responsible fire-safety professionals, including occupants, visitors, staffing and night-time conditions. Consider personal emergency evacuation plans (PEEPs) or other individual arrangements where applicable, suitable warning methods and any designed refuges or evacuation lifts. Confirm the actual assistance and communication process; a refuge or plan document alone is not an evacuation solution. Do not assume an ordinary lift is available during fire.

- [ ] **ACCOM-15 (C/N)** Make emergency instructions understandable and available through the appropriate communication routes. Any voice messages, signs and illuminated guidance must support the site's approved strategy and safe escape routes, not automatically direct everyone to the nearest door. Do not alter an alarm, evacuation plan or emergency system on the strength of this checklist.

## 3. The rooms

- [ ] **ACCOM-16 (P/N)** Apply ACCOM-08 to room entrances and internal doors. Verify furniture, bathroom approaches, door swings, privacy, assisted access and emergency release where required. Do not prescribe inward opening for every room or bathroom; decide door configuration and closing/hold-open behaviour through the applicable access and fire-safety design.

- [ ] **ACCOM-17 (P/C)** Offer quieter/lower-stimulation room choices where feasible, considering lifts, plant, bins, kitchens, external activity, noise and odours. Confirm the person's preferences and actual room conditions rather than assigning a room automatically from a diagnosis.

- [ ] **ACCOM-18 (P)** Plan manoeuvring, parking and safe mobility-equipment charging without blocking escape. Verify bed/bathroom transfer space for the selected layout and occupants' needs; consider left- and right-transfer choices and bed approach from both sides. Include hoist or other equipment compatibility where needed, with competent assessment and safe operating arrangements.

- [ ] **ACCOM-19 (P)** Do not assume that an unusually low fixed bed suits every transfer. Establish an appropriate height, support, stability and adjustment option for the scoped needs; offer double beds where appropriate. Equipment choice and transfer practice require competent advice and individual preferences, not an improvised agent recommendation.

- [ ] **ACCOM-20 (P)** Plan nearby personal-assistant (PA) or companion accommodation where requested, with a connecting-door option where appropriate. Confirm availability, privacy, staffing and booking arrangements without assuming everyone needs or wants a companion.

- [ ] **ACCOM-21 (P/N)** Place and verify essential storage, switches, sockets, blinds/windows, mirrors, washbasins, toilet paper, shower controls and assistance controls within the specified reach and use conditions. Consider seated users, people of short stature and limited dexterity; use suitable easy-grip fittings. Assess toilet-paper reach without unnecessary stretching or rotation and retain clear transfer spaces.

- [ ] **ACCOM-22 (C/N)** Offer suitable lighting adjustment, glare control, blackout options, ventilation, acoustic separation and restrained visual patterns. Assess actual flicker, noise and control behaviour rather than treating a lamp technology or “no neon” label as a sufficient specification. Support a dark, quiet recovery environment where feasible without disabling safety systems or excluding the occupant's other needs.

- [ ] **ACCOM-23 (P/C)** Provide usable kitchen circulation, reachable appliances and easy-grip utensils or other aids where relevant. Agree an accessible process for requesting them. Where needed, plan an assistance-dog resting area and bowls without obstructing routes or assuming that an app is required to identify cupboards or appliances.

- [ ] **ACCOM-24 (C/N)** Supply an accessible room guide explaining layout, controls, emergency arrangements, routes and support/adjustment requests. Offer appropriate text, visual, spoken or other formats and an effective assistance route. Confirm how Deaf or hard-of-hearing occupants receive relevant announcements, contact staff and obtain a response.

- [ ] **ACCOM-25 (P)** Do not make the evacuation arrangements depend on an occupant operating firefighting equipment. Where equipment is provided, the responsible professionals must specify its use, accessibility and training. Follow the site's strategy; this checklist does not instruct occupants to fight a fire.

## 4. Assistive technology, alarms and commissioning

- [ ] **ACCOM-26 (N/P)** Specify media systems and controls that support the required captions, audio description, usable menus, adjustable presentation and accessible remote operation for the actual service. Verify discoverability and operation on the installed equipment. Keep the remote in a reachable, consistently located place agreed for the room; being close to the television is not enough if the occupant cannot reach it from bed or seating.

- [ ] **ACCOM-27 (N)** Where a person chooses phone-based door/object detection, check current device/OS support and manufacturer limitations. Apple states that Magnifier Detection Mode should not be relied on for navigation or high-risk/emergency situations. Do not make the building's accessibility or safety depend on successful camera recognition or require occupants to own an iPhone. Use reliable physical information and an alternative assistance route.

- [ ] **ACCOM-28 (N/C)** Consider optional location labels and remote visual-assistance services, including NaviLens or Be My Eyes, where appropriate. Verify the actual connectivity needed rather than assuming one Wi-Fi design guarantees access. Consider privacy, cost, service availability and consent before sharing images of people or private rooms. Provide a usable alternative when a device, network or external service is unavailable.

- [ ] **ACCOM-29 (P/N/C)** Smart lighting, electronic maps, Bluetooth and voice controls can add useful choice. Essential functions also need an accessible fallback appropriate to the occupant and building. Test loss of Wi-Fi, phone, app and relevant power dependencies; a physical control is not automatically accessible to every person. Never alter safety-system behaviour through a consumer automation workaround.

- [ ] **ACCOM-30 (N/C)** Have fire-warning provision specified and checked within the site's fire strategy, including audible, visual and tactile/vibrating options as appropriate for the actual occupants and sleeping conditions. Do not reduce or disable alarms to avoid sensory shock. Agree familiarisation and appropriate support for sensory-sensitive people without relying on one warning modality or claiming a consumer device is a compliant substitute.

- [ ] **ACCOM-31 (P/N)** Provide emergency assistance controls that are reachable and usable in relevant positions, including the bed, toilet, shower and floor, as applicable. Keep pull cords or other controls unobstructed and verify the entire alert, acknowledgement and response process. An installed alarm is not evidence that assistance will arrive; confirm responsible staff, coverage and failure arrangements.

- [ ] **ACCOM-32 (P/N/C)** Plan full-journey evaluation with people who use the relevant access routes, including disabled and neurodivergent participants with varied needs and preferences. Cover representative arrival, room use, rest, support and relevant daytime/night-time conditions. Emergency rehearsals or simulations must be agreed, risk-assessed and supervised by competent people; do not expose participants to hazardous scenarios to demonstrate accessibility. Record untested journeys, observations, participant accounts, barriers, corrections and retest evidence separately. One participant or a favourable average cannot establish suitability for everyone.

## Recording an assessment

Record the item ID, actual source revision, project/room/route, applicability and reason, accepted criterion and its source, observation or claim, method/date/conditions, result, limitations, owner, next action and decision authority where known. Keep **verified**, **failed**, **unknown**, **not tested** and **not applicable** distinct. Record open decisions as **unresolved** with a next action; do not substitute them for test results. Keep **supplier claim**, **observed condition**, **participant account**, **agent inference** and **human approval** separate. Installation and checklist completion are not equivalent to an achieved occupant outcome.

Do not publish personal needs, diagnoses, contact details, individual emergency plans or identifiable participant feedback in this repository or a public dataset. Use the responsible organisation's approved arrangements.
